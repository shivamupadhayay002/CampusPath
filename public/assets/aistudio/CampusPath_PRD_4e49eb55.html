CampusPath

A student-focused community platform for sharing knowledge, discovering opportunities, building connections and participating in college life.

Web applicationResponsiveStudent communityMVP → Scale

1. Product overview

Product name: CampusPath

Product type: College-focused social networking and student community platform.

Vision: Give students one trusted digital place to connect with peers, faculty and alumni; exchange academic resources; discover internships and opportunities; and share their progress.

Initial launch: One college. The product should be designed so additional colleges can be added later without rebuilding the platform.

Product principle: CampusPath is not intended to be a general-purpose public social network. Its initial community, discovery and access rules are designed around verified college membership.

2. Problem statement and goals

Problems to solve

  • Academic notes, useful links and learning resources are scattered across chats and groups.
  • Students often miss internships, events, referrals and placement updates.
  • Students have limited ways to discover helpful peers, seniors, professors and alumni.
  • Useful project experiences and interview preparation advice are difficult to find later.

Product goals

  • Make sharing and discovering college-relevant content simple.
  • Help students build meaningful academic and professional connections.
  • Make opportunities and study resources searchable and organized.
  • Provide safe communication and practical reporting controls.
  • Launch a reliable MVP for one college, then validate demand before expanding.

Non-goals for the first release

  • Public, unrestricted social networking across the internet.
  • Complex recommendation algorithms or creator monetization.
  • Video calling, live streaming, stories with advanced editing, or a native mobile app.
  • Automated claims that a LinkedIn profile alone proves college enrollment.

5. Functional requirements

5.1 Registration, login and student verification

  • Users can register, sign in, sign out and recover access.
  • Registration collects only necessary information, such as name, email, college and course details.
  • Where supported, verify college email ownership using a one-time verification link or code.
  • Provide an administrator-reviewed verification route for students whose college email cannot be verified automatically.
  • LinkedIn connection may be offered as an optional profile connection or professional identity signal. It must not be treated as proof of current student status.
  • Unverified users must have clearly limited access until verification is complete.

5.2 Profiles

  • Members can edit profile photo/avatar, display name, college, branch, academic year, bio, skills, projects and achievements.
  • Profiles show appropriate activity such as posts and public achievements.
  • Users can control which optional profile fields are visible to other members.
  • Support profile reporting and blocking.

5.3 Feed and posts

  • Show posts relevant to the member's college and followed accounts.
  • Support text, category, tags and optional attachments.
  • Allow like/unlike, comment, save/unsave and share-copy-link actions.
  • Allow post owners to edit or delete their own posts.
  • Provide pagination or incremental loading rather than loading the entire feed at once.
  • Validate text length, file type and file size on the server.

5.4 File and media uploads

  • Support approved image formats and PDF/document uploads in the MVP.
  • Video upload can be enabled as a later release after storage, bandwidth, moderation and playback costs are assessed.
  • Show upload progress, success and failure states.
  • Store files privately by default where content is restricted to college members; use controlled access links.
  • Scan or validate uploads where suitable tools are available. Never trust a filename or browser-provided MIME type alone.

5.5 Search and discovery

  • Search people, post titles, descriptions, tags and opportunity listings.
  • Filter by category, college, branch or relevant tags where permitted.
  • Do not expose private profile fields or private posts through search.

5.6 Follow and social interactions

  • Members can follow and unfollow permitted profiles.
  • Prevent duplicate likes, follows and repeated interaction records.
  • Display counts consistently and update them from trusted backend data.

5.7 Messaging

  • Support one-to-one text conversations between permitted members.
  • Show conversation list, message history, sent time and unread state.
  • Enforce conversation membership on every read and send operation.
  • Provide block and report controls and reasonable rate limits.
  • Do not imply end-to-end encryption unless it has actually been implemented and independently assessed.

5.8 Notifications

  • Notify users about relevant follows, comments, mentions and messages.
  • Allow users to mark notifications as read.
  • Provide notification preferences where practical.

5.9 Saved posts and achievements

  • Members can save and remove saved posts.
  • Members can add, edit and remove their own achievements.
  • Achievement labels and points must not imply official academic certification unless verified.

5.10 Admin dashboard

  • Review verification requests and reported content.
  • View member status and moderation history according to assigned permissions.
  • Remove or restrict content/accounts with a recorded reason.
  • Manage college records, categories and moderator roles.
  • Maintain an audit log for sensitive administrative actions.

6. Trust, safety and moderation

Safety is a launch requirement, not an optional enhancement. The platform must provide clear rules and a way to act on violations.

Required controls

  • Report post, comment, profile and message.
  • Block a member and prevent unwanted contact where technically possible.
  • Rate limits and anti-spam controls for registration, posting, comments and messaging.
  • Moderator queue with status, priority, reason and resolution notes.
  • Clear community guidelines covering harassment, threats, impersonation, scams, sexual exploitation, harmful content and copyright infringement.
  • Age-appropriate access policy and a process to handle accounts that do not meet platform eligibility rules.
  • Appeal or review process for significant moderation decisions.

Verification and privacy

  • Collect the minimum information needed to establish eligibility.
  • Do not display student ID documents or verification evidence publicly.
  • Restrict access to verification records to authorized administrators.
  • Define retention and deletion periods for verification evidence.
  • Never claim that a user is verified solely because they have a LinkedIn account.
Operational requirement: Before public launch, publish community rules, privacy information, a contact/reporting route and an incident-response procedure. Obtain appropriate legal review for the intended users and operating locations.

7. UX and visual design requirements

  • Use the supplied CampusPath.html as the visual reference for the product's initial design language.
  • Retain the CampusPath wordmark and purple/pink/orange gradient identity.
  • Use a clean, rounded-card interface with readable typography and consistent spacing.
  • Support responsive desktop, tablet and mobile layouts.
  • Desktop layout: top navigation, left profile/sidebar, central feed and right discovery sidebar.
  • Mobile layout: compact top bar and fixed bottom navigation.
  • Provide light and dark themes, accessible contrast, visible focus states and keyboard navigation.
  • Every interactive control must have loading, success, error and empty states as appropriate.
  • Use confirmation for destructive actions such as deleting a post or account.

Visual direction

Primary accent: #5B4BFF · Pink: #DD2A7B · Orange: #F58529 · Font direction: Plus Jakarta Sans or a comparable accessible sans-serif.

8. Data and technical architecture

The implementation may be generated with Google AI Studio, but the delivered application must use a clear and maintainable architecture. Keep UI, business logic, server-side authorization and data access separated.

Suggested technology direction

LayerSuggested optionNotes
FrontendReact + TypeScriptReusable components and typed application logic.
StylingTailwind CSSConsistent responsive design system.
Backend / dataSupabase or an equivalent managed backendDatabase, authentication, storage and realtime features.
DatabasePostgreSQLRelational data with constraints and indexes.
HostingSuitable static/app hosting providerChoose based on framework and backend requirements.
Source controlGitHubVersion history, branches and code review.

Core data entities

  • users / profiles: account identifier, display name, role, college and profile fields.
  • colleges: college identity, approved email domains and status.
  • verification_requests: status, review metadata and restricted evidence references.
  • posts: author, category, text, tags, visibility and timestamps.
  • post_attachments: storage reference, file type, size and access policy.
  • comments, likes, follows, saved_posts: interaction records with uniqueness constraints.
  • conversations, conversation_members, messages: private messaging records.
  • notifications: recipient, type, source and read state.
  • reports, moderation_actions, audit_logs: safety workflow and administrative traceability.

Backend security rules

  • Enable row-level access policies or equivalent server-side authorization for every private table.
  • Users may modify only records they own, except authorized moderation/admin actions.
  • Conversation messages are visible only to conversation members and authorized processes.
  • Storage access must follow post/profile visibility and membership rules.
  • Never rely on frontend route guards as the only security control.
  • Keep service-role keys and other secrets on trusted server-side infrastructure.

10. Success metrics

Track product usefulness and platform health. Do not optimize only for time spent or raw post volume.

  • Activation: percentage of registered members who complete onboarding and verification.
  • Engagement: weekly active members, meaningful posts, comments and resource saves.
  • Utility: study resources opened/downloaded and opportunity posts viewed.
  • Community health: reports per active member, time to review reports and repeat abuse rate.
  • Reliability: error rate, page performance, failed uploads and message delivery failures.
  • Retention: members who return in the following week or month.

Set numerical targets after collecting baseline data from the first pilot. Avoid inventing targets before usage is understood.

12. MVP acceptance criteria

  • A new eligible user can register, verify their college membership and complete a profile.
  • An unauthorized or unverified user cannot access restricted college content.
  • A member can create, edit and delete their own post.
  • Other permitted members can view a post and interact with it.
  • Search and category filters return relevant, authorized results.
  • File uploads enforce server-side type and size restrictions and respect access permissions.
  • A member can follow another member, save a post and manage those lists.
  • Private messages cannot be read by users outside the conversation.
  • Members can report and block accounts or content.
  • Moderators can review reports and record actions.
  • Administrative actions are permission-controlled and auditable.
  • Core screens work on supported mobile and desktop browsers.
  • Account deletion and data handling follow the published policy.
  • Critical security tests and backup/restore checks are completed before the pilot.

13. Risks, assumptions and open decisions

Risks

  • Fake student accounts: mitigate with college email verification and a documented manual review route.
  • Abusive content or contact: mitigate with reporting, blocking, moderation staffing and rate limits.
  • Data exposure: mitigate with backend authorization tests, private storage and limited administrator access.
  • Hosting and media costs: set file limits, monitor usage and review pricing before enabling video at scale.
  • AI-generated code defects: require code review, automated tests and manual security checks before deployment.
  • Low initial adoption: run a focused pilot and prioritize the features students actually use.

Assumptions

  • The first deployment serves one college community.
  • The first version is a responsive website, not a native mobile application.
  • Students, faculty and alumni may have different role permissions.
  • LinkedIn is optional and is not the source of truth for college enrollment.
  • Video support is not required for the first MVP unless separately prioritized.

Decisions to confirm before implementation

  1. Which college(s) and email domains are included in the first pilot?
  2. Will faculty and alumni be enabled in the first release or added later?
  3. Who will act as moderator and how quickly should reports be reviewed?
  4. What file types and maximum upload sizes are allowed?
  5. What minimum age and eligibility policy will the platform enforce?
  6. Which hosting and backend plans meet the expected pilot usage and budget?
